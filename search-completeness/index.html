Search completeness · registry vs literature miss rate

A literature-only search systematically misses registered trials — they were never published (ghosts), or their paper is indexed under a different condition (a T2D trial not found under "obesity"). Against a registry cohort (CT.gov NCT IDs) + the trial→publication linkage, this quantifies how many your search missed, the trial-to-publication linkage rate (~63.6% baseline), and the denominator-bias factor. Report it so a review of N publications isn't mistaken for "all trials".

Caveats

This measures retrieval completeness against the registry, not absolute truth. The registry cohort itself may be incomplete (not all trials register); "ghost" requires knowing a trial is truly unpublished (registration ≠ completion); and linkage depends on the registry's recorded publications (AACT study_references), which lag. Use the miss rate + denominator factor as an explicit missing-evidence term — it complements ROB-ME / reporting-bias, it doesn't replace them.

A registered trial counts as "found" if any of its linked PMIDs is in your search hits. sensitivity = found / cohort; denominator factor = 1/sensitivity (a search of N papers ≈ N/sensitivity registered trials); linkage rate = trials with ≥1 linked publication / cohort. Misses are categorised: unpublished ghost, published-but-not-retrieved, or no publication link. Idea integrated from the glp1-obesity-mbnma literature arm (registry-vs-MEDLINE).
