Small-study-effect tests

Small-study tests include Egger (unweighted regression, multiplicative dispersion, t test with k-2 degrees of freedom), Begg-Mazumdar rank correlation, Peters and Harbord for binary outcomes, and the Thompson-Sharp random-effects (REML) meta-regression test. Trim-and-fill uses L0 and REML. Numerical agreement depends on inputs and convergence; these methods do not establish publication bias on their own.

PET–PEESE and leave-one-out sensitivity

PET and PEESE are weighted regressions of the effects on their standard errors (PET) or variances (PEESE), weights 1/variance, with t inference on k−2 degrees of freedom; the intercept is the limit estimate for a study with zero standard error. PET is the same weighted model as the Egger test above. Conditional rule: use PEESE when PET’s intercept test rejects at two-sided 5% (p < 0.05), otherwise PET. Leave-one-out repeats every validated test with each study omitted in turn and flags omissions that move a p-value across 0.05, change the trim-and-fill k0 or change the PET–PEESE branch. These are sensitivity analyses; they do not establish publication bias.

Tests: Egger (continuous), Begg-Mazumdar rank correlation, Harbord-Egger modification for binary OR with log-score transform, Peters' test for log-OR using 1/(total sample size) as predictor, Thompson-Sharp as a REML random-effects meta-regression on the standard error (z test, as metafor regtest model="rma"; agrees with metafor 5.2-1 on all 152 corpus configurations, checked by "Validate the extras in R"). See Sterne JAC, Sutton AJ, Ioannidis JPA, et al. BMJ 2011;343:d4002 for funnel-plot recommendations and power comparisons. Egger reports a t statistic, not a z statistic. The selection model uses ML and is experimental, not validated against metafor.
