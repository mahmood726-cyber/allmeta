GOSH plot — all-subset meta-analysis

Graphical display of study heterogeneity (Olkin, Dahabreh, Trikalinos 2012). For every non-empty subset of studies, compute the pooled estimate and I² under a fixed-effect or DL random-effects model. Each subset becomes one dot; clusters suggest influential studies or subgroup structure. For k > 15 the engine uses random sampling (advanced-stats.md).

Interpretation: multimodal clustering indicates subgroup heterogeneity (the meta-analysis averages two or more distinct populations). A thick “horizontal band” at one estimate means most subsets converge on the same answer — homogeneous evidence. Streaks along the I² axis often indicate one or two highly influential outliers.
Olkin I, Dahabreh IJ, Trikalinos TA. GOSH — a graphical display of study heterogeneity. Res Synth Methods 2012;3(3):214–23. Subgroup structure interpretation aligns with Cochrane Handbook v6.5 §10.10.2 on detecting influential studies; I² interpretation per §10.10.2 (low <30%, moderate 30–60%, substantial 50–90%, considerable 75–100%). For k > 15 this app draws 5,000 subsets by default, choosing sizes uniformly from 2 to k, with replacement, using a fixed seed. This differs from metafor::gosh(), which enumerates all non-empty subsets when 2^k - 1 is at most 1,000,000 (k up to 19), and otherwise draws 1,000,000 subsets by default with binomial size probabilities. Full enumeration here includes single-study subsets.
