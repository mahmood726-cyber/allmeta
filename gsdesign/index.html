Group-Sequential Design

Efficacy and optional β-spending futility boundaries for a K-look group-sequential trial, computed with the same numerical method as the R package gsDesign (Jennison–Turnbull recursive integration), and Monte Carlo operating characteristics of the design, with optional sample-size re-estimation, following the R package rpact. Unlike the meta-analysis monitoring tools (TSA, sequential-MA), these are trial-design boundaries. Runs offline; nothing leaves the browser.

Design

Nominal p is the one-sided (or, for two-sided tests, two-sided) p-value at the efficacy bound. Information is reported relative to a fixed (single-analysis) design with the same α and power.

Adaptive simulation — two-group comparison of means

Uses the design above (one-sided only): efficacy bounds as critical values, futility bounds as non-binding stopping rules, information fractions as planned stage proportions. Normal approximation, 1:1 allocation, re-estimation as in rpact::getSimulationMeans.

Method. Sequential Z-statistics with independent increments; crossing probabilities by the recursive numerical integration of Jennison & Turnbull (2000, ch. 19) on their grid (r = 18, Simpson weights); bounds by the Newton iteration and maximum information by Brent root finding, reproducing gsDesign::gsDesign (test.type 1–4). Spending: Lan & DeMets (1983) O'Brien-Fleming and Pocock types, Kim-DeMets power family, Hwang-Shih-DeCani (1990). Simulation: stage-wise z-statistics, inverse normal or pooled test statistic, and conditional-power sample-size re-estimation as in rpact::getSimulationMeans. Engine shared/gsdesign.js. Ported from the author's AdaptSim. The design can be shared as a link: inputs are stored in the address after #.
