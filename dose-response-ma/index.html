Dose-Response Meta-Analysis

Pools dose-response curves from studies that report relative risks at several doses against a reference. The correlation between estimates that share a reference group is reconstructed from the counts (Greenland–Longnecker or Hamling). Curves can be linear, quadratic or restricted cubic splines, fitted by the two-stage or one-stage approach. The estimation reproduces the R package dosresmeta. Distinct from network dose-response.

Engine: shared/dose-response.js (fitDR), a port of the estimation in dosresmeta 2.2.0 and mixmeta. Doses are measured from each study's reference dose. The two-stage approach fits each study by generalised least squares and pools the coefficient vectors: REML and ML start from 10 iterative GLS steps, then maximise with BFGS. The one-stage approach fits all points together with a random-effects covariance, as dosresmeta does (Nelder–Mead, capped at 100 likelihood evaluations; the app says when the cap stops it before convergence). A two-stage fit needs as many non-reference doses in every study as the curve has coefficients; otherwise the app refuses, as dosresmeta does, and suggests the one-stage approach. Q, I², the Wald tests, the goodness-of-fit statistics (deviance, R², from gof()) and the decorrelated residuals follow dosresmeta's definitions.

Verification: every multi-study binary dataset shipped with dosresmeta (16 datasets, 724 model × covariance × approach × method combinations) is reproduced within numerical tolerance, and every fit dosresmeta refuses is refused (hub/shared/tests/dose-response-dosresmeta-parity.spec.mjs).

References: Greenland & Longnecker 1992, Am J Epidemiol 135:1301; Orsini, Bellocco & Greenland 2006, Stata J 6:40; Hamling et al. 2008, Stat Med 27:954; Orsini et al. 2012, Am J Epidemiol 175:66; Gasparrini, Armstrong & Kenward 2012, Stat Med 31:3821; Crippa & Orsini 2016, J Stat Softw 72(c01); Harrell 2015, Regression Modeling Strategies.
