Surrogate validation · trial-level surrogacy

Is a treatment's effect on a surrogate outcome (weight loss, HbA1c, LDL…) a valid stand-in for its effect on the final outcome (MACE, mortality)? Trial-level surrogacy regresses the final-outcome effects on the surrogate effects across trials. This reports the naive R², the estimation-error-adjusted R² (Daniels-Hughes — strips the within-trial noise off the final side), leave-one-out robustness, and a surrogate threshold effect — and refuses the adjusted R² when the final effects don't vary enough to estimate it (the trap a naive R² hides).

Caveats

Trial-level surrogacy ≠ individual-level surrogacy, and a high R² is necessary but not sufficient. It needs enough trials with real between-trial variation in BOTH effects; with small k or clustered final effects the adjusted R² is unstable (flagged here). The surrogate threshold effect is a point estimate (predicted-null crossing), not a licensing threshold. Validate the slope is stable (leave-one-out) and confirm biological plausibility before treating a surrogate as a substitute endpoint.

Weighted regression of the final effects on the surrogate effects (weights 1/SE²); naive R² = weighted R². Adjusted R² (Daniels-Hughes / van Houwelingen) = cov(s,f)² / (var(s)·(var(f)−mean SE²)), clipped to 1, and refused when Q≤k−1 or the adjusted final variance ≤ 25% of observed (too little signal). I²(final) shows the between-trial signal; STE = −intercept/slope. Cross-language validated against numpy. Idea integrated from the glp1-obesity-mbnma surrogate arm. Everything runs on your device.
