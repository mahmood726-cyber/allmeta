Reporting-bias / missing-evidence cockpit

Funnel plots and Egger's test are inputs, not the verdict. The formal question — ROB-ME (Page et al. 2023, Cochrane Handbook §13) — is whether the synthesis is at risk of bias because evidence is missing: whole studies absent (publication bias) or results selectively unreported within studies. This cockpit quantifies the missing-evidence denominator and outcome-switching, computes small-study asymmetry, and scaffolds a ROB-ME-aligned judgement.

1 · Missing-study denominator

Across registries, only ~63.6% of registered trials publish usable results (TrialScout 2026); a synthesis of N studies often draws on a denominator of ~1.6N. Count abstracts/registrations/theses you found but could not include.

2 · Outcome-switching diff

One outcome per line; matched case-insensitively. ~24% of pre-specified outcomes are silently dropped and ~52% of trials show a protocol-vs-paper discrepancy (medRxiv 2025).

3 · Small-study asymmetry (Egger) — an input

Egger's regression intercept ≠ 0 signals funnel asymmetry. Low power for k<10; asymmetry has many causes besides reporting bias — it is one input to the judgement, never the verdict.

Framework, paraphrased. These prompts follow the ROB-ME structure (4 within-study + 4 across-study signalling questions; Page et al. BMJ 2023;383:e076754). Overall risk of bias due to missing evidence:
Judgement, not a score. Low = result unlikely to be biased by missing evidence; High = likely biased; Some concerns = uncertainty precludes either. The suggestion here is a non-binding cue from the data panels and your responses; the reviewer decides per the ROB-ME guidance.
