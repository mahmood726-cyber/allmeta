New to meta-analysis? Use this three-step preparation guide, then open the full workspace. Already familiar with the methods? Use the direct launch link above. This guide does not alter analysis settings or upload your data.

Step 1 of 3 · Prepare study data

Choose your outcome format

Binary: for each study, enter treatment events, treatment total, control events and control total. Available measures: OR, RR, RD.

Use one record per independent study comparison. Check missing denominators, zero cells and duplicated participants before fitting.

Step 2 of 3 · Set the question

Define the estimand and direction

Specify which arm is treatment and whether larger or smaller values represent benefit. OR, RR and HR have a null of 1; MD, SMD and RD have a null of 0.

In the workspace, explicitly inspect the pooling model, heterogeneity estimator (such as REML or Paule–Mandel), interval method (including HKSJ where offered), continuity correction and prediction interval assumptions.

Do not pool incompatible effect scales or overlapping cohorts as independent studies.

Step 3 of 3 · Check and preserve

Review your result before use

Inspect individual study estimates and forest/funnel plots, consider heterogeneity and sensitivity analyses, then rerun after changing inputs or settings.

Record the source rows, estimator, correction rules, result date and version separately. The current analysis JSON export is not yet verified as a complete reproducibility record for every outcome type.

A successful plot or built-in validation check is not independent scientific certification.

Why do these choices matter?

Different corrections, effect orientations, tau² estimators and confidence-interval conventions can change clinical interpretations. For example, when there are few studies, a Hartung–Knapp confidence interval can differ from a conventional normal interval. Verify results against a matched reference analysis for important decisions.
