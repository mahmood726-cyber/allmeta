CINeMA — Confidence in NMA

Rate each pairwise comparison in a network meta-analysis across six CINeMA domains (Nikolakopoulou et al., PLOS Med 2020): within-study bias, reporting bias, indirectness, imprecision, heterogeneity, incoherence. The page shows a suggested summary from the paper's optional rule (start at High; drop 1 level per domain with some concerns or suspected reporting bias, 2 per domain with major concerns). The final rating is the reviewer's judgement: where concerns are related, the paper advises against downgrading more than once, so you can set the final rating, with a stated reason.

Suggested summary: Nikolakopoulou et al. 2020 (PLOS Med 17:e1003082) say reviewers "may then choose" to summarise judgements across the six domains with the four GRADE levels, for example by starting at High and dropping 1 level for each domain with Some concerns and 2 levels for each domain with Major concerns. That optional rule is what the Suggested column shows (floor Very low). Reporting bias has the paper's two descriptions, Undetected and Suspected; the paper gives no numeric mapping for them, so on this page Suspected reporting bias counts as one level (as Some concerns), in line with GRADE's single-level downgrade when publication bias is suspected. Related concerns: the paper asks reviewers to avoid downgrading more than once for related concerns (its example: some concerns for imprecision and heterogeneity and major concerns for incoherence; the tally gives Very low, yet 2 levels are sufficient, i.e. Low). The summary is the reviewer's judgement, so set the Final rating where that applies; an override needs a reason, which is shown in the table and the CSV export. "Unclear" is not a CINeMA level: a comparison with any unresolved domain is shown as Not fully assessed and gets no rating. Domain judgements are entered by you here. In the official CINeMA web app (cinema.ispm.unibe.ch) the reviewer's study-level input on within-study bias and indirectness is combined with the network data and user-defined rules to assign each domain's level, and its Report tab lets users not downgrade, or downgrade by 1 or 2 levels each relative treatment effect; the paper cautions against relying naively on the software's programmed rules.
