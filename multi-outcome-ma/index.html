Multi-outcome meta-analysis (bivariate REML)

Pool two correlated outcomes jointly rather than separately. The bivariate random-effects model uses REML to estimate the 2×2 between-study covariance Σ_RE (τ₁, τ₂, ρ_between), then borrows strength across outcomes via the within-study correlation ρ_within. Especially valuable when (i) outcomes are correlated, (ii) some studies report only one outcome (Riley 2007 "missing-at-random" gains), or (iii) the secondary outcome is sparse. The side-by-side panel shows per-outcome univariate REML vs the joint bivariate fit so you can see the CI tightening from borrowing.

Borrowing-of-strength: when ρ_between is non-zero, the bivariate model uses information from outcome 2 to refine the pooled estimate of outcome 1 (and vice-versa). Studies that report only one outcome still contribute through this correlation channel — that's the Riley 2007 gain.
