Transitivity & representativeness · NMA assumption screening

Two generalisability checks that network meta-analyses usually leave to prose. Transitivity screens whether effect modifiers are distributed similarly across the network's comparisons (the core NMA validity assumption — if a modifier differs across comparisons they aren't exchangeable). Representativeness compares the trial population to a target population per modifier, making external validity explicit. A screen for the reviewer to judge, not a hypothesis test.

How to read this

This screens; it does not decide. Transitivity uses the relative spread (coefficient of variation) of each modifier's node means — a flag means "inspect this", not "transitivity is violated"; CV is scale-relative, so judge clinical importance (a small CV can still be a clinically large gap, e.g. HbA1c). Unmeasured modifiers may still differ. Representativeness uses the standardised difference vs the target; a flag means the evidence may not transport without adjustment (see the Transportability app).

Transitivity: per modifier, the distribution of node means across the network; flag when CV > 0.15. Representativeness: per modifier, the standardised difference (Δ/target SD, or relative Δ when no SD); flag when |std diff| > 0.5 (or |relΔ| > 0.15).
