Transported NMA · population-relevant network meta-analysis

A standard NMA ranks treatments in the source network's case-mix. If your target population has a different distribution of effect modifiers, that ranking may not transport. This entropy-balances each study so the network's weighted modifier means match a target profile (Hainmueller), reweights the random-effects NMA, and shows the source vs target league and P-score ranking — plus the effective sample size the transport costs, which tells you whether the answer is interpolation or extrapolation.

Caveats — what transport does and does not fix

Transport corrects only for the modifiers you supply. Unmeasured effect modifiers and modifier × treatment interactions are not corrected. Entropy balancing matches the target moments exactly when the target lies inside the studies' covariate range; outside it, it extrapolates — watch the effective sample size (ESS) and the achieved-vs-target gap. The variance-inflation reweighting is an aggregate-data approximation to a full IPD network meta-regression (conservative for down-weighted studies). A reordered ranking is a hypothesis about the target population, not a definitive result — read the source league alongside it.

Entropy balancing: Hainmueller (2012) — weights closest to uniform whose weighted covariate means equal the target. Reweighted random-effects NMA reuses the audited WLS network solver (Paule–Mandel τ²); P-scores: Rücker & Schwarzer (2015). ESS = Kish 1/Σwᵢ². Method integrated from the nmatransport project.
