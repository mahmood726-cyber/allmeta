Spec-collapse

A multiverse meta-analysis runs many analytic specifications on one dataset. The usual way to summarise the spec-curve — inverse-variance pooling the specification estimates as if independent — collapses the confidence interval by roughly the number of specs and manufactures robustness the data don't support. This tool runs your dataset across a 36-specification grid and compares that anti-conservative naive pool against a coverage-calibrated weighted-likelihood interval (never narrower than a single spec). Across 473 Cochrane reviews, 55% of "robust" conclusions collapsed to fragile under the correct method.

Studies

The rule (advanced-stats): never IV-RE-pool many-analyst / multiverse results from one dataset — the CI collapses below truth. Under the null, the naive pool's Type-I error runs 70–81% (nominal 5%); the weighted-likelihood interval holds 94–99% coverage.

Method

Each of the 36 specs (3 τ² estimators × 2 CI methods × 3 outlier rules × {raw, trim-and-fill}) yields a pooled effect θ̂ₛ and variance Vₛ via the audited ma-core / trimfill. The naive IV-RE pool takes variance 1/Σ(1/Vₛ) — collapsing by ~S. The weighted-likelihood summary is the mixture Σ pₛ · t-density(θ̂ₛ, Vₛ, kₛ−1); by the law of total variance its variance is mean-within + between-spec, so it is never narrower than the average single spec. Interval via numeric quantile inversion of the mixture CDF.

Port of the Spec-Collapse Atlas weighted-likelihood aggregator (externally validated vs metafor across 473 Cochrane reviews; Pages: mahmood726-cyber.github.io/spec-collapse-atlas). Method after Wagenmakers-style likelihood combination; the IV-RE-collapse caution follows the project's advanced-stats rule. Per-spec pooling and trim-and-fill use the audited shared/ma-core.js and shared/trimfill.js; the aggregators are in shared/spec-collapse.js and cross-checked against the Python engine in tests/test_spec_collapse.py. Computed client-side; data stays in your browser.
