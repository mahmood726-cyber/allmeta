# Departure ledger — every non-literal reading of a rule, logged

**Warrant: the Cochrane Handbook itself.** Not our invention, and not an escape hatch we
built for ourselves. Fetched, not recalled — **Handbook v6.5, Chapter 1 "Starting a
review", last updated August 2021**:

> *"Preparing a systematic review is complex and **involves many judgements**. To
> minimize the potential for bias in the review process, these judgements should be made
> as far as possible **in ways that do not depend on the findings** of the studies
> included in the review."*

> *"**Post hoc decisions** made when the impact on the results of the research is known,
> such as excluding selected studies from a systematic review, or changing the
> statistical analysis, are **highly susceptible to bias and should therefore be avoided
> unless there are reasonable grounds** for doing this."*

> *"Changes to the methods outlined in the protocol should be **transparently
> declared**."*

> *"**Transparent reporting of review decisions** enables readers to assess the
> reliability of the review for themselves."*

**So the tension dissolves.** "Apply the rules sensibly" **is** what Cochrane says to do —
the field's own book resists mechanical application. But the Handbook attaches precisely
the structure that keeps it falsifiable: judgement **independent of the findings**,
post-hoc decisions **avoided unless justified**, departures **transparently declared**.
Those are the same three rules, arrived at independently. We are not bending the
standard; we are following it.

---

## The three mechanical rules

1. **Departures are allowed. SILENT departures are not.** Every row below logs: the rule,
   the reading rejected, the reading adopted, the reason, and **the direction it moves
   our answer**.
2. **Declared BEFORE the result, or marked POST-HOC.** Both permitted; different
   evidential weight; labelled differently. A post-hoc departure is a **hypothesis**, not
   a finding. *(This is the Handbook's own distinction, above.)*
3. ⭐ **THE LEDGER TEST — does a departure ever COST us?** If every "sensible" reading we
   adopt happens to favour our conclusion, it is not judgement, it is bias with better
   manners. **A departure that hurts our result is the evidence our judgement is real.**
   **If the ledger comes out one-sided, that is a finding about US and gets reported.**

## Consequence for the contamination audit (B3)

The Handbook supplies the standard directly: *"Changes to the methods outlined in the
protocol should be transparently declared."*

⇒ **The defect is not the departure. The defect is the departure nobody declared.**

- **B3a CONTAMINATION** = pooled trial violates the meta's own stated criteria, **with no
  declared rationale** → a Handbook violation on its own terms.
- **B3b DECLARED BROAD INCLUSION** = the meta says it includes phase 2 (or the broader
  population), gives a rationale, and does so → **exactly the judgement the Handbook
  asks for. Not an error. Not counted against them.**

This is the same standard we now hold ourselves to in this ledger — which is what makes
it fair to apply to them.
