Registry publication bias · measure, don't just infer

Egger / trim-fill / Copas can only infer missing evidence from the published funnel — low power, and they can't tell true suppression from look-alike small-study heterogeneity. The registry observes the posted-but-unpublished "ghost" trials, so the bias is measured directly: pool the published set vs the registry-complete set, and read the shift. That ground truth disambiguates the funnel inference — catching both missed bias and spurious trim-fill "corrections".

Caveats

This measures bias against the registry, not absolute truth. It only works when ghost trials posted enough data to pool; truly dark trials (no posted results) remain unmeasured (and the registry cohort may itself be incomplete). The "measured shift" uses a Paule-Mandel RE pool; "meaningful" means the registry-complete mean leaves the published 95% CI. The disambiguation is the value: an Egger flag with a negligible measured shift is heterogeneity, not suppression — don't trim-and-fill it.

Pools via the audited shared/ma-core.js (Paule-Mandel τ²); funnel asymmetry via shared/egger.js (p<0.10 flag). Classification: measured-bias (ghosts shift the pool beyond published precision), spurious-asymmetry (Egger flags but the observed ghosts barely move it — a trim-fill correction would be wrong), no-bias, or inference-only (no ghosts supplied).
