Transportability · generalise an effect to your population

Standard meta-analysis gives the average effect across the trials. This estimates the effect in your target population when an effect modifier (baseline risk, mean age, % with diabetes, BMI…) differs from the trials: a random-effects meta-regression on the modifier, predicting the mean effect at your population's modifier value, with a Knapp-Hartung CI and an unmeasured-modifier sensitivity. An aggregate, IPD-free transport (one-step-NMR idea). It is assumption-laden — read the assumptions panel before trusting it.

Assumptions (read before using)

Transport is valid only if: (1) the modifier you entered is the (dominant) effect modifier and is measured on the same scale in trials and target; (2) no unmeasured effect modifier also differs between trials and target; (3) the linear meta-regression is a reasonable model over the modifier range; (4) you are not extrapolating far beyond the trials' modifier range. The sensitivity below quantifies how much an unmeasured shift would nullify the result, but cannot prove there isn't one.

Method: random-effects meta-regression (Paule-Mandel τ²; Knapp-Hartung tk−2 with the HKSJ q = max(1, RSS/(k−2)) floor) fitted on the modifier, then the mean effect predicted at the target modifier value with the regression covariance. Cross-language validated against SciPy (transportability/tests/). This is an aggregate, IPD-free transport — the one-covariate sibling of ML-NMR / one-step network meta-regression; population-mean transport (the prediction-interval variant for a single new trial adds τ² and is wider). Everything runs on your device.
