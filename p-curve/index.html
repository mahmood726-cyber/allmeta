p-curve & p-uniform

Publication-bias-robust effect estimation from the distribution of statistically significant p-values. A right-skewed p-curve indicates evidential value; a flat or left-skewed curve is consistent with no true effect or selective reporting.

p-curve: Simonsohn U, Nelson LD, Simmons JP. p-curve: a key to the file-drawer. J Exp Psychol Gen 2014;143:534-547. Method-vs-code disclosure (allmeta heuristic): the right-skew test here uses Fisher's combined method -2·Σ ln(pp) ~ χ²(2k) on uniformly-transformed pp = p/0.05 values (the original Simonsohn 2014 paper recommends Stouffer's z-pool; both detect right-skew but power differs). The flatness test is Simonsohn's real 33%-power test: for each significant study a pp-value is computed under a 33%-power alternative (the non-centrality giving that study's test 1/3 power at α=.05, two-sided) and the pp-values are combined by Stouffer's method Z = ΣΦ⁻¹(pp)/√k; a significantly small flatness p means the curve is flatter than 33% power (inadequate evidential value). Full and half (p<.025) curves are reported. The non-central benchmark is computed for the two families the app accepts as input — two-sided z (ncp⃗ μ ≈ 1.529) and non-central t at the common df (CDF verified against scipy.stats.nct to 1e-6); every input row is therefore covered. (A legacy binomial pp≤0.5 screen is retained in the exported JSON only, for schema back-compat, and is not shown.) p-uniform: van Assen MALM, van Aert RCM, Wicherts JM. Psychol Methods 2015;20:293-309.
