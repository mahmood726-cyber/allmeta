p-curve & p-uniform

Publication-bias-robust effect estimation from the distribution of statistically significant p-values. A right-skewed p-curve indicates evidential value; a flat or left-skewed curve is consistent with no true effect or selective reporting.

Input

Evidential value tests (p-curve)

p-uniform effect size

p-curve distribution (significant p only)

p-curve: Simonsohn U, Nelson LD, Simmons JP. p-curve: a key to the file-drawer. J Exp Psychol Gen 2014;143:534-547. Simonsohn U, Simmons JP, Nelson LD. Better p-curves. J Exp Psychol Gen 2015;144:1146-1152. Methods: inputs are two-sided p-values; 0 < p < .05 are used. Right skew is tested by Fisher's method on pp = p/.05 (the 2014 paper) and by Stouffer's method Z = ΣΦ⁻¹(pp)/√k on pp = 20p (full) and 40p (half, p<.025), as in the p-curve app 4.x. Flatness is the 33%-power test of the app 4.x: the non-centrality giving 1/3 two-sided power at α=.05 (normal for z; non-central t at the common df), pp33 conditional on p<.05 (full) or p<.025 (half), combined by Stouffer; a small flatness p means the curve is flatter than 33% power. pp-values are bounded to [2.2×10⁻¹⁶, 1−2.2×10⁻¹⁶] as in the app 4.x. Binomial tests and the power estimate of the app 4.x are not computed. p-uniform: van Assen MALM, van Aert RCM, Wicherts JM. Psychol Methods 2015;20:293-309 — computed as puniform 0.2.8 puniform(yi = z, vi = 1, side = "right", method = "P") with z = Φ⁻¹(1 − p/2) (estimate, Irwin-Hall 95% CI, test of no effect; δ in z-units). Normal, t and non-central t distributions are ports of the R nmath algorithms. (A legacy binomial pp≤0.5 screen is retained in the exported JSON only, for schema back-compat, and is not shown.)
