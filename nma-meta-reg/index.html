Network meta-regression (treatment × covariate)

Network meta-regression with REML (default), ML, or fixed-effect estimation, and a separate interaction slope for each non-reference treatment. A multi-arm study must supply all m(m−1)/2 pairwise contrasts (as netmeta requires; a study with only its baseline contrasts is refused); the fit then uses its contrasts against the study baseline, with random-effect correlation 0.5. The model centres the covariate at the mean of retained rows, so the beta table reports treatment effects at that mean. Predictions account for covariance between effects and slopes.

Contrast rows + study-level covariate

Use a covariate likely to MODIFY treatment effect (mean age, baseline risk, year, dose). The γ̂ tells you whether the effect changes with the covariate — and how much. Always inspect the prediction plot to see whether the effect modification is clinically meaningful across the observed covariate range.

Engine: shared/nma-meta-regression.js. REML (default), ML, or FE as in netmeta::netmetareg(consistency=TRUE, assumption="independent"). Random effects have rho = 0.5 within multi-arm studies. A multi-arm study must supply all m(m−1)/2 pairwise contrasts, as netmeta requires (a study given only its baseline contrasts is refused with "wrong number of comparisons"); the fit then uses only the contrasts against the study baseline, as netmetareg, oriented relative to that baseline. Within-study sampling covariance is not modelled because input is contrast level. Single numeric study-level covariate per fit. A row trtA, trtB, yi means yi is the effect of trtB relative to trtA: in netmeta use treat1 = trtB, treat2 = trtA, TE = yi. Coefficients at covariate 0 and slopes then equal netmetareg's; the beta table here is at the mean covariate xbar of retained rows. Validated against netmeta 3.7-0 netmetareg (see the paper-28 validation archive).
