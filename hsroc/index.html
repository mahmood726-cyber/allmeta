Bivariate DTA / HSROC

Meta-analysis of diagnostic test accuracy: bivariate random-effects model on logit(Se) and logit(FPR = 1−Sp), with a parametric HSROC curve and summary operating point. Fitted by full maximum-likelihood bivariate random effects (Reitsma), matching R's mada::reitsma for k ≥ 4; a DerSimonian-Laird per-margin approximation is retained only as a small-sample fallback when k < 4.

Model: bivariate random-effects on logit-Se, logit-FPR, correlation ρ — Reitsma JB et al. J Clin Epidemiol 2005;58:982–990; Rutter CM, Gatsonis CA. Stat Med 2001;20:2865–2884 (HSROC). Fitted by maximum-likelihood bivariate random effects for k ≥ 4 via the shared Reitsma engine (Cholesky-parameterised Σ, GLS-profiled μ, Nelder-Mead; mada::reitsma-parity to ~1e-4). A univariate DerSimonian-Laird per-margin approximation (Harbord RM et al. Biostatistics 2007;8:239–251) is retained only as a k < 4 fallback where Σ is not identifiable. 0.5 continuity correction when any cell is zero. HSROC curve = locus of (FPR, Se) pairs from the conditional mean linear predictor. Choosing a tool: for the Moses SROC line and a Spearman threshold-effect check (e.g. k<5 or when bivariate fails to converge), use Moses DTA-SROC →.
